# Italian C1 Syllabus --- Advanced / Effective Operational Proficiency

## Level objective

Use Italian flexibly, fluently and precisely across social, academic and
professional contexts, including implicit meaning and nuanced argument.

## How the tutoring agent should use this file

Teach one or a small cluster of competencies at a time. Begin with
retrieval of previously learned material, give a concise explanation and
examples, require the learner to produce Italian, then test the same
competency in a different context. Track recurring errors explicitly. Do
not equate completing a lesson with mastery.

When testing, mix: - comprehension/recognition; - fill or transformation
tasks; - short-answer production; - translation only where useful for
diagnosing form; - role-play; - free conversation; - short writing; -
cumulative review of older competencies.

Correct errors selectively during fluency work: prioritize errors that
block meaning, are central to this CEFR level, or recur. After
correction, make the learner produce a new sentence using the corrected
pattern.

## Core grammar and structures

-   Full command and stylistic choice across indicative, conditional and
    subjunctive systems
-   Complex sequence of tenses
-   Advanced hypothetical and counterfactual structures
-   Marked word order and information structure
-   Cleft and dislocation structures
-   Advanced passive, impersonal and nominal styles
-   Dense relative and subordinate clauses
-   Advanced connective repertoire
-   Register-dependent pronoun and address choices
-   Idiomatic pronominal verbs
-   Lexical aspect and fine verb-choice distinctions
-   Formal written syntax and punctuation
-   Rhetorical mitigation, emphasis and hedging
-   Reported discourse and viewpoint management

## Core communicative topics

-   Professional negotiation and presentations
-   Academic argument
-   Complex social and cultural issues
-   Literary and film criticism
-   Media rhetoric
-   Public debate
-   Specialist topics within the learner's own domain
-   Humour, irony and implied meaning
-   Regional and register variation
-   Formal correspondence
-   Abstract ethics and philosophy
-   Long-form Italian media

## Things the learner should be able to say/do

-   Give a 10-minute structured presentation with nuanced Q&A
-   Argue both sides of a complex issue before stating a qualified
    position
-   Summarize a long text while preserving stance and nuance
-   Rephrase the same message for a friend, colleague and formal
    institution
-   Interpret implied meaning, irony or rhetorical framing
-   Explain a specialist concept precisely to expert and non-expert
    audiences
-   Negotiate diplomatically and soften disagreement
-   Write a coherent analytical essay with nuanced transitions

## Example proficiency questions

These are prompts, not a fixed exam. The agent should generate fresh
variants and follow-up questions.

-   In che misura il linguaggio influenza il modo in cui interpretiamo
    la realtà?
-   Presenta due interpretazioni plausibili dello stesso fenomeno
    sociale e confrontane i presupposti.
-   Riformula una critica dura in modo diplomatico senza perderne il
    contenuto.
-   Spiega un argomento complesso del tuo settore prima a un esperto e
    poi a un adolescente.
-   Analizza il tono e le strategie retoriche di un editoriale o di un
    discorso.
-   Racconta un episodio facendo emergere implicitamente, senza dirlo in
    modo diretto, il tuo giudizio.
-   Quali sfumature cambiano tra *credo che*, *ritengo che*, *mi pare
    che* e *a mio avviso*?
-   Sostieni una tesi, poi confutala nel modo più convincente possibile.

## Common mistakes to watch for

-   Grammatically correct but pragmatically unnatural phrasing
-   Register inconsistency inside the same text or conversation
-   Overuse of learned idioms where native usage would be plainer
-   Weak collocational precision
-   Missing subtle tense/mood implications
-   Excessive explicitness where Italian discourse prefers implication
    or pronoun reference
-   English-influenced rhetorical structure
-   Failure to hedge appropriately in professional disagreement
-   Repetition caused by insufficient synonym/collocation range
-   Treating every acceptable sentence as equally natural

## Error handling protocol

For each meaningful recurring error: 1. Record the learner's original
wording. 2. Give the corrected form. 3. Explain the rule in the shortest
useful way. 4. Ask for 2-3 fresh examples, not repetition of the
corrected sentence. 5. Re-test later in a different context. 6. Escalate
to `recurring_errors` if it appears repeatedly. 7. Do not block fluency
to correct every minor error.

Suggested state:

``` yaml
error:
  category: grammar|vocabulary|pronunciation|register|word_order|collocation
  learner_form: ""
  corrected_form: ""
  explanation: ""
  occurrences: 1
  last_seen: null
  status: active  # active | improving | resolved
```

## Suggested lesson loop

1.  **Recall (5-10%)** --- review due items and one recurring mistake.
2.  **Teach (15-20%)** --- one clear concept with natural examples.
3.  **Controlled practice (20%)** --- transformations, completion, short
    answers.
4.  **Active production (25%)** --- learner creates original sentences
    or a mini-dialogue.
5.  **Interaction (20%)** --- role-play or conversation where the target
    form is useful but not explicitly cued.
6.  **Feedback (5-10%)** --- identify 1-3 high-value corrections.
7.  **Persist state** --- update evidence, errors and review queue.

## Mastery evidence

For an individual competency, seek evidence on more than one attempt. A
useful default: - recognition: \>= 80% on a small mixed check; -
controlled production: correct on several fresh prompts; - free
production: used correctly without the structure being named; -
retention: demonstrated again after a delay; - recurring-error check: no
unresolved systematic error that contradicts mastery.

These percentages are tutoring heuristics, not official CEFR scoring
rules.

## Level exit checkpoint

-   Can participate effectively in demanding professional or academic
    interaction
-   Can understand extended speech even when structure is not explicit
-   Can read long complex texts and identify stance, implication and
    rhetorical choices
-   Can write clear, well-structured, stylistically appropriate complex
    texts
-   Can deliberately shift register and reformulate with little loss of
    nuance
-   Errors are occasional and usually self-corrected; they do not reveal
    persistent gaps in core grammar

### Exit test design

The agent should build a mixed assessment rather than a grammar
checklist: - a spontaneous conversation; - at least one
role-play/problem-solving task; - a reading task with comprehension and
reaction; - a listening task when audio is available; - a short writing
task; - targeted probes for the learner's recurring errors; - several
unannounced opportunities to use key grammar.

If performance is uneven, keep the current level and identify the
smallest set of competencies preventing promotion. If the learner
consistently meets the exit criteria, update persisted state to the next
CEFR level while continuing spaced review of earlier material.
