# Italian B2 Syllabus --- Upper-intermediate / Vantage

## Level objective

Interact with substantial spontaneity, understand complex standard
language, and develop and defend viewpoints in detail.

## How the tutoring agent should use this file

Teach one or a small cluster of competencies at a time. Begin with
retrieval of previously learned material, give a concise explanation and
examples, require the learner to produce Italian, then test the same
competency in a different context. Track recurring errors explicitly. Do
not equate completing a lesson with mastery.

When testing, mix: - comprehension/recognition; - fill or transformation
tasks; - short-answer production; - translation only where useful for
diagnosing form; - role-play; - free conversation; - short writing; -
cumulative review of older competencies.

Correct errors selectively during fluency work: prioritize errors that
block meaning, are central to this CEFR level, or recur. After
correction, make the learner produce a new sentence using the corrected
pattern.

## Core grammar and structures

-   Confident indicative tense system
-   Present and past subjunctive
-   Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive
-   Sequence of tenses with the subjunctive
-   Real, possible and unreal conditional periods
-   Conditional for reported/unconfirmed information
-   Passive voice and si constructions
-   Advanced relative clauses
-   Pronoun combinations and clitic placement
-   Advanced uses of ci and ne
-   Reported speech
-   Nominalisation and more formal syntax
-   Concessive, causal, consequential and contrastive connectors
-   Common idioms, collocations and pronominal verbs
-   Register-sensitive alternatives to basic vocabulary

## Core communicative topics

-   Workplace and professional communication
-   Education and public life
-   Media and misinformation
-   Environment and policy at general-interest level
-   Technology and AI
-   Culture and criticism
-   Travel and intercultural experiences
-   Social trends
-   Economics and consumer life at non-specialist level
-   Ethics and dilemmas
-   Italian institutions and society
-   Debate and argumentation

## Things the learner should be able to say/do

-   Present a structured argument with supporting reasons and examples
-   Defend a viewpoint under follow-up questioning
-   Speculate about causes and consequences
-   Summarize and react to a substantial article or interview
-   Tell a detailed story with precise temporal relationships
-   Handle disagreement politely and reformulate a point
-   Give a formal-ish presentation on a familiar professional topic
-   Write a reasoned essay, review or formal complaint

## Example proficiency questions

These are prompts, not a fixed exam. The agent should generate fresh
variants and follow-up questions.

-   È meglio lavorare per vivere o vivere per lavorare? Argomenta la tua
    posizione.
-   In che modo la tecnologia ha cambiato le relazioni personali?
-   Se i social network non fossero mai esistiti, che cosa sarebbe
    diverso oggi?
-   Riassumi un articolo e distingui i fatti dalle opinioni dell'autore.
-   Quali misure potrebbero rendere una città più vivibile?
-   Racconta un evento complesso usando chiaramente cause, conseguenze e
    retroscena.
-   Non sono d'accordo con te: difendi la tua idea e poi riconosci un
    punto valido dell'altra posizione.
-   Spiega un concetto del tuo lavoro o dei tuoi studi a una persona non
    esperta.

## Common mistakes to watch for

-   Using indicative after structures that conventionally require
    subjunctive
-   Tense-sequence mismatches in past reported thought/speech
-   Incorrect mixed conditional periods
-   Excessive reliance on generic words: *cosa, fare, bello, importante*
-   English-influenced collocations despite grammatically correct
    sentences
-   Register that is too casual for formal writing
-   Overlong sentences without clear logical structure
-   Misplacing clitics with infinitives, imperatives and gerunds
-   Failing to distinguish certainty, possibility, opinion and hearsay
    linguistically
-   Speaking fluently but repeatedly fossilising small
    agreement/preposition errors

## Error handling protocol

For each meaningful recurring error: 1. Record the learner's original
wording. 2. Give the corrected form. 3. Explain the rule in the shortest
useful way. 4. Ask for 2-3 fresh examples, not repetition of the
corrected sentence. 5. Re-test later in a different context. 6. Escalate
to `recurring_errors` if it appears repeatedly. 7. Do not block fluency
to correct every minor error.

Suggested state:

``` yaml
error:
  category: grammar|vocabulary|pronunciation|register|word_order|collocation
  learner_form: ""
  corrected_form: ""
  explanation: ""
  occurrences: 1
  last_seen: null
  status: active  # active | improving | resolved
```

## Suggested lesson loop

1.  **Recall (5-10%)** --- review due items and one recurring mistake.
2.  **Teach (15-20%)** --- one clear concept with natural examples.
3.  **Controlled practice (20%)** --- transformations, completion, short
    answers.
4.  **Active production (25%)** --- learner creates original sentences
    or a mini-dialogue.
5.  **Interaction (20%)** --- role-play or conversation where the target
    form is useful but not explicitly cued.
6.  **Feedback (5-10%)** --- identify 1-3 high-value corrections.
7.  **Persist state** --- update evidence, errors and review queue.

## Mastery evidence

For an individual competency, seek evidence on more than one attempt. A
useful default: - recognition: \>= 80% on a small mixed check; -
controlled production: correct on several fresh prompts; - free
production: used correctly without the structure being named; -
retention: demonstrated again after a delay; - recurring-error check: no
unresolved systematic error that contradicts mastery.

These percentages are tutoring heuristics, not official CEFR scoring
rules.

## Level exit checkpoint

-   Can converse at length with native speakers without requiring
    unusual accommodation
-   Can argue, qualify, speculate, disagree and reformulate
    spontaneously
-   Can understand the main content and much detail in standard
    broadcasts and discussions
-   Can read contemporary non-specialist prose independently
-   Can produce clear, detailed, well-structured writing
-   Uses the subjunctive and conditional systems productively, with
    errors no longer systematic

### Exit test design

The agent should build a mixed assessment rather than a grammar
checklist: - a spontaneous conversation; - at least one
role-play/problem-solving task; - a reading task with comprehension and
reaction; - a listening task when audio is available; - a short writing
task; - targeted probes for the learner's recurring errors; - several
unannounced opportunities to use key grammar.

If performance is uneven, keep the current level and identify the
smallest set of competencies preventing promotion. If the learner
consistently meets the exit criteria, update persisted state to the next
CEFR level while continuing spaced review of earlier material.
