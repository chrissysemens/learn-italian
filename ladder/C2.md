# Italian C2 Syllabus --- Mastery

## Level objective

Understand virtually everything encountered and express subtle
distinctions spontaneously, idiomatically and with precise control of
register and rhetoric.

## How the tutoring agent should use this file

Teach one or a small cluster of competencies at a time. Begin with
retrieval of previously learned material, give a concise explanation and
examples, require the learner to produce Italian, then test the same
competency in a different context. Track recurring errors explicitly. Do
not equate completing a lesson with mastery.

When testing, mix: - comprehension/recognition; - fill or transformation
tasks; - short-answer production; - translation only where useful for
diagnosing form; - role-play; - free conversation; - short writing; -
cumulative review of older competencies.

Correct errors selectively during fluency work: prioritize errors that
block meaning, are central to this CEFR level, or recur. After
correction, make the learner produce a new sentence using the corrected
pattern.

## Core grammar and structures

-   Grammar as stylistic choice rather than topic coverage
-   Fine-grained mood and tense effects
-   Marked constructions for emphasis, rhythm and stance
-   Ellipsis and implicit reference
-   Highly idiomatic clitic and pronominal usage
-   Literary and formal syntactic patterns
-   Productive manipulation of register
-   Discourse-level cohesion over long texts
-   Rhetorical parallelism, irony, understatement and implication
-   Variation between written, spoken, formal, colloquial and regional
    forms
-   Precision in punctuation and prosody
-   Recognition of archaic, literary and bureaucratic constructions
    without requiring active imitation

## Core communicative topics

-   Dense literary and essayistic prose
-   Fast unscripted conversation
-   Professional and academic specialist discourse
-   Satire, humour and wordplay
-   Regional varieties and sociolinguistic variation
-   Rhetoric and persuasion
-   Translation and reformulation
-   Stylistic editing
-   Complex cultural references
-   Debate with shifting perspectives
-   Long-form synthesis across sources
-   Near-native pragmatic judgment

## Things the learner should be able to say/do

-   Summarize several complex sources into one coherent synthesis
-   Convey the same proposition with irony, diplomacy, neutrality and
    force
-   Interpret humour or wordplay and explain why it works
-   Edit an advanced text for rhythm, register and idiomaticity
-   Participate in rapid multi-speaker discussion and accurately report
    competing positions
-   Translate or reformulate meaning while preserving tone rather than
    wording
-   Distinguish near-synonyms and explain their pragmatic effect
-   Produce extended speech that sounds natural rather than merely
    correct

## Example proficiency questions

These are prompts, not a fixed exam. The agent should generate fresh
variants and follow-up questions.

-   Spiega la differenza pragmatica tra varie formulazioni quasi
    sinonimiche scelte dal tutor.
-   Riassumi un dibattito complesso attribuendo con precisione ogni
    posizione.
-   Riformula un testo burocratico in italiano naturale, poi trasformalo
    in registro formale elegante.
-   Analizza un brano per ironia, implicature, ritmo e scelte lessicali.
-   Difendi una tesi usando un registro accademico, poi ripresentala in
    una conversazione informale.
-   Individua le frasi grammaticalmente corrette ma poco idiomatiche in
    un testo avanzato e migliorale.
-   Spiega una battuta o un gioco di parole senza limitarti alla
    traduzione letterale.
-   Produci una sintesi che distingua chiaramente fatti,
    interpretazioni, obiezioni e concessioni.

## Common mistakes to watch for

-   Assuming C2 means knowing every word or never making mistakes
-   Choosing a technically correct word with the wrong connotation or
    collocation
-   Missing irony, understatement, cultural implication or speaker
    stance
-   Register choices that reveal translation rather than native-like
    formulation
-   Overformalising ordinary conversation
-   Using regional or slang expressions without awareness of social
    meaning
-   Losing attribution when synthesising several speakers or sources
-   Subtle reference ambiguity in long discourse
-   Rhythm and punctuation that make advanced writing feel translated
-   Failing to notice that two grammatically possible forms communicate
    different attitudes

## Error handling protocol

For each meaningful recurring error: 1. Record the learner's original
wording. 2. Give the corrected form. 3. Explain the rule in the shortest
useful way. 4. Ask for 2-3 fresh examples, not repetition of the
corrected sentence. 5. Re-test later in a different context. 6. Escalate
to `recurring_errors` if it appears repeatedly. 7. Do not block fluency
to correct every minor error.

Suggested state:

``` yaml
error:
  category: grammar|vocabulary|pronunciation|register|word_order|collocation
  learner_form: ""
  corrected_form: ""
  explanation: ""
  occurrences: 1
  last_seen: null
  status: active  # active | improving | resolved
```

## Suggested lesson loop

1.  **Recall (5-10%)** --- review due items and one recurring mistake.
2.  **Teach (15-20%)** --- one clear concept with natural examples.
3.  **Controlled practice (20%)** --- transformations, completion, short
    answers.
4.  **Active production (25%)** --- learner creates original sentences
    or a mini-dialogue.
5.  **Interaction (20%)** --- role-play or conversation where the target
    form is useful but not explicitly cued.
6.  **Feedback (5-10%)** --- identify 1-3 high-value corrections.
7.  **Persist state** --- update evidence, errors and review queue.

## Mastery evidence

For an individual competency, seek evidence on more than one attempt. A
useful default: - recognition: \>= 80% on a small mixed check; -
controlled production: correct on several fresh prompts; - free
production: used correctly without the structure being named; -
retention: demonstrated again after a delay; - recurring-error check: no
unresolved systematic error that contradicts mastery.

These percentages are tutoring heuristics, not official CEFR scoring
rules.

## Level exit checkpoint

-   Can understand virtually all ordinary and demanding spoken/written
    Italian, including implicit meaning, given reasonable access to
    context
-   Can synthesize multiple complex sources accurately and economically
-   Can express fine shades of meaning and reformulate immediately when
    needed
-   Can control register, rhetoric, idiomaticity and interpersonal
    nuance
-   Can identify and repair subtle unnaturalness, not only grammatical
    error
-   Remaining gaps are domain-, culture- or exposure-specific rather
    than a general proficiency deficit

### Exit test design

The agent should build a mixed assessment rather than a grammar
checklist: - a spontaneous conversation; - at least one
role-play/problem-solving task; - a reading task with comprehension and
reaction; - a listening task when audio is available; - a short writing
task; - targeted probes for the learner's recurring errors; - several
unannounced opportunities to use key grammar.

If performance is uneven, keep the current level and identify the
smallest set of competencies preventing promotion. If the learner
consistently meets the exit criteria, update persisted state to the next
CEFR level while continuing spaced review of earlier material.
