# Italian B1 Syllabus --- Intermediate / Threshold

## Level objective

Function independently in most everyday situations and produce connected
language about experiences, plans, opinions and events.

## How the tutoring agent should use this file

Teach one or a small cluster of competencies at a time. Begin with
retrieval of previously learned material, give a concise explanation and
examples, require the learner to produce Italian, then test the same
competency in a different context. Track recurring errors explicitly. Do
not equate completing a lesson with mastery.

When testing, mix: - comprehension/recognition; - fill or transformation
tasks; - short-answer production; - translation only where useful for
diagnosing form; - role-play; - free conversation; - short writing; -
cumulative review of older competencies.

Correct errors selectively during fluency work: prioritize errors that
block meaning, are central to this CEFR level, or recur. After
correction, make the learner produce a new sentence using the corrected
pattern.

## Core grammar and structures

-   Secure present, passato prossimo and imperfetto
-   Trapassato prossimo
-   Future simple and future perfect at a practical level
-   Conditional present and past
-   Present subjunctive in frequent triggers
-   Imperative with pronouns
-   Combined object pronouns
-   Expanded uses of ci and ne
-   Relative pronouns: che, cui, quello che
-   Si impersonale and si passivante
-   Gerund and infinitive constructions
-   Common discourse connectors
-   Pronominal verbs and common idiomatic verb constructions
-   Basic reported speech and tense relationships

## Core communicative topics

-   Education and careers
-   Relationships and social life
-   Travel problems and experiences
-   Culture, film, books and music
-   News and current events at accessible level
-   Environment and everyday sustainability
-   Technology and social media
-   Health and lifestyle in non-specialist contexts
-   Consumer issues
-   Goals, decisions and hypothetical situations
-   Italian society and regional life
-   Personal opinions and preferences

## Things the learner should be able to say/do

-   Narrate an experience with background, sequence and outcome
-   Explain and justify an opinion
-   Describe a problem and negotiate a solution
-   Summarize the plot of a film/book and react to it
-   Discuss advantages and disadvantages of a familiar issue
-   Talk about hopes, plans and hypothetical choices
-   Retell information received from another person
-   Write an informal complaint or structured personal email

## Example proficiency questions

These are prompts, not a fixed exam. The agent should generate fresh
variants and follow-up questions.

-   Raccontami un viaggio in cui qualcosa è andato storto.
-   Quali sono i vantaggi e gli svantaggi di lavorare da casa?
-   Se avessi più tempo libero, che cosa faresti?
-   Parlami di un film o libro che ti è piaciuto e spiegami perché.
-   Che cosa pensi dei social media? Quali problemi possono creare?
-   Qual è una decisione importante che hai preso?
-   Che cosa speri di fare nei prossimi cinque anni?
-   Riassumi una notizia recente che hai capito in italiano.

## Common mistakes to watch for

-   Avoiding the subjunctive entirely after frequent triggers
-   Overusing passato prossimo where narrative background requires
    imperfetto
-   Confusing conditional and imperfect in hypothetical language
-   Pronoun order errors in combined pronouns
-   Literal English phrasal patterns and collocations
-   Overusing *che* where *cui* or another construction is needed
-   Run-on speech with too few connectors
-   Register mismatch between tu and Lei
-   False friends such as *attualmente*, *eventualmente*, *parenti*
-   Using correct grammar only in exercises but reverting to A2
    structures in free production

## Error handling protocol

For each meaningful recurring error: 1. Record the learner's original
wording. 2. Give the corrected form. 3. Explain the rule in the shortest
useful way. 4. Ask for 2-3 fresh examples, not repetition of the
corrected sentence. 5. Re-test later in a different context. 6. Escalate
to `recurring_errors` if it appears repeatedly. 7. Do not block fluency
to correct every minor error.

Suggested state:

``` yaml
error:
  category: grammar|vocabulary|pronunciation|register|word_order|collocation
  learner_form: ""
  corrected_form: ""
  explanation: ""
  occurrences: 1
  last_seen: null
  status: active  # active | improving | resolved
```

## Suggested lesson loop

1.  **Recall (5-10%)** --- review due items and one recurring mistake.
2.  **Teach (15-20%)** --- one clear concept with natural examples.
3.  **Controlled practice (20%)** --- transformations, completion, short
    answers.
4.  **Active production (25%)** --- learner creates original sentences
    or a mini-dialogue.
5.  **Interaction (20%)** --- role-play or conversation where the target
    form is useful but not explicitly cued.
6.  **Feedback (5-10%)** --- identify 1-3 high-value corrections.
7.  **Persist state** --- update evidence, errors and review queue.

## Mastery evidence

For an individual competency, seek evidence on more than one attempt. A
useful default: - recognition: \>= 80% on a small mixed check; -
controlled production: correct on several fresh prompts; - free
production: used correctly without the structure being named; -
retention: demonstrated again after a delay; - recurring-error check: no
unresolved systematic error that contradicts mastery.

These percentages are tutoring heuristics, not official CEFR scoring
rules.

## Level exit checkpoint

-   Can sustain a 7-10 minute unrehearsed conversation on familiar
    subjects
-   Can narrate, explain, compare and justify with connected discourse
-   Can cope with most travel and everyday problems without switching
    language
-   Can understand the main points of clear standard media on familiar
    topics
-   Can write structured texts with paragraphs and linking devices
-   Shows emerging spontaneous use of conditional, pronouns and common
    subjunctive triggers

### Exit test design

The agent should build a mixed assessment rather than a grammar
checklist: - a spontaneous conversation; - at least one
role-play/problem-solving task; - a reading task with comprehension and
reaction; - a listening task when audio is available; - a short writing
task; - targeted probes for the learner's recurring errors; - several
unannounced opportunities to use key grammar.

If performance is uneven, keep the current level and identify the
smallest set of competencies preventing promotion. If the learner
consistently meets the exit criteria, update persisted state to the next
CEFR level while continuing spaced review of earlier material.
